# Coronary Heart Disease Risk Factors

## Overview

Coronary heart disease (CHD) develops when the coronary arteries become narrowed or blocked, reducing blood flow to the heart. Several modifiable risk factors significantly increase the likelihood of developing CHD. Understanding these factors allows healthcare providers to identify high-risk patients and recommend preventive interventions.

## Major Risk Factors

### High Blood Pressure

Systolic blood pressure (SBP) is one of the strongest modifiable risk factors for coronary heart disease. Blood pressure measures the force of blood against artery walls. Persistently elevated SBP damages blood vessels, accelerates plaque formation, and increases the heart's workload.

Clinical guidelines generally classify SBP below 120 mmHg as normal, while values of 130 mmHg or greater are associated with increased cardiovascular risk.

### High Cholesterol

Non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (non-HDL-C) includes all cholesterol particles capable of contributing to plaque buildup in arteries. Elevated cholesterol increases the risk of atherosclerosis, heart attack, and stroke.

Lowering LDL cholesterol through lifestyle changes or statin therapy has been shown to substantially reduce future cardiovascular events.

### Diabetes

Diabetes damages blood vessels through chronically elevated blood glucose levels. Individuals with diabetes are significantly more likely to develop coronary artery disease and often experience cardiovascular complications at younger ages than individuals without diabetes.

Maintaining healthy blood glucose levels can reduce long-term cardiovascular risk.

### Smoking

Smoking damages the lining of blood vessels, promotes inflammation, increases blood clot formation, and reduces oxygen delivery throughout the body. Even moderate smoking substantially increases the likelihood of coronary heart disease.

Smoking cessation remains one of the most effective interventions for reducing cardiovascular risk.

## Population Impact

Large observational studies involving more than 22,000 participants have shown that controlling major risk factors such as blood pressure and cholesterol could substantially reduce coronary heart disease events across the population.

For example:

- Maintaining SBP below 130 mmHg may significantly reduce future cardiovascular events.
- Lowering LDL cholesterol by approximately 30% can meaningfully decrease 10-year coronary heart disease risk.

Although each individual risk factor contributes only modestly to predictive models, together they represent the majority of preventable cardiovascular disease.

## Key Takeaways

- High blood pressure is one of the strongest modifiable risk factors.
- Elevated cholesterol contributes to plaque formation.
- Diabetes accelerates vascular damage.
- Smoking dramatically increases cardiovascular risk.
- Managing these risk factors through medication and lifestyle changes can substantially reduce future coronary heart disease.
